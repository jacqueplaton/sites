### What this suggests (interpretation)

- The market sells these five components **separately** or as **build + retainer**.
  The search found no clearly comparable offer that is all five, one-time, with a
  formal handover. That is an absence in a small search, not proof of a gap.
- Buyer pain worth validating in sales calls: recurring fees with unclear output,
  accounts owned by the agency, ads launched without tracking, leads not answered.
  These appeared as themes in agency content, not in buyer research.
- Speed-to-lead statistics ("5 minutes", "21x") circulate widely but trace back
  to a 2011 study of web leads across industries. Do not use them in copy.

### Unknowns

- Real demand for a no-retainer implementation in this segment.
- Whether owners will actually run Google Ads themselves after handover.
- Jacque's location, delivery capacity, track record and preferred tool stack.
- Competitor contract terms and real pricing (quote-only).

## 2. Recommended segment and geography

**Recommendation — segment:** established, owner-led home-service and residential
contracting companies (remodeling, roofing, HVAC, plumbing, electrical), roughly
5–30 staff, already winning work by referral.

Why: job values are high enough that one recovered inquiry is material; demand is
search-driven and local, so Google Business Profile and Search ads are the natural
channels; crews are on site and calls get missed; and the incumbent vendors serving
this segment sell on recurring contracts, which gives a one-time, client-owned build
a clear point of difference.

**Recommendation — geography:** United States, delivered remotely, with outreach
starting in Florida metro areas. Florida is an assumption drawn only from the other
project in this repository; replace it with Jacque's real home market.

## 3. The offer

**Name:** The Front Door Build
**Category line:** Acquisition and lead-handling infrastructure, built once and handed over.

### Customer profile and qualification

Qualifies when all are true:

1. Trading two years or more, with capacity for more work.
2. Typical job value of roughly $1,500 or more (or strong repeat value).
3. A named person answers new leads in business hours.
4. Can fund advertising after launch on their own card (suggest $1,500+/month; validate).
5. One decision-maker, feedback within three business days.
6. Accepts that they operate the system after handover.

Disqualify: wants monthly management, wants guaranteed results, pre-launch,
multi-location or franchise, needs e-commerce or custom software.

### Problem and value proposition

Problem (to validate per prospect): the pieces that carry an inquiry from search to
follow-up were set up separately and never connected or tested together.

Value proposition: one fixed-scope project that builds and connects all five pieces,
proves them with test leads, trains the team and leaves every account in the client's
name. The promise is a working, tested, documented system — not an outcome.

### Deliverables and scope limits

| Component | Problem addressed | Delivered | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Business website | Unclear positioning | Messaging brief; copy, design and build; on-page basics and local structured data; forms and click-to-call wired to CRM | Up to 7 pages; 1 copy review, 2 design reviews |
| B. Conversion landing page | Weak conversion path | One page for the priority service, one action, message-matched to ads, tracked | 1 page, 1 service |
| C. Google Business Profile | Local visibility and trust | Claim or clean-up, categories, services, area, hours, description, photos, tracked link, review-request routine and templates | 1 location; verification is Google's decision |
| D. Google Ads | Poor tracking | Account in client's name, form and call conversion tracking tested end to end, one Search campaign, operating guide | 1 campaign, up to 3 ad groups |
| E. CRM, routing, follow-up | Missed inquiries, inconsistent follow-up | Pipeline, lead capture with source, assignment and notification rules, workflows, one contact import | 1 pipeline, 6 stages, 3 users, 5 workflows, 1 CSV import |

Excluded: ongoing ads management, SEO campaigns, content, social, branding,
photography, video, e-commerce, booking engines, custom software, extra locations
or platforms, Local Services Ads, legal texts, data migration beyond one CSV.

### Client responsibilities

Decision-maker and three-day feedback; account access or creation in week one;
real photos, licence and insurance details; own payment method for ads and software;
lead handlers present at both trainings; messaging consent and carrier registration.

### Stages and timeline (proposed: six weeks)

| When | Stage | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Before kickoff | Implementation Assessment | Written findings, confirmed scope, fixed fee, third-party cost estimate |
| Week 1 | Blueprint | Positioning, priority service, lead rules, access |
| Weeks 2–4 | Website and landing page | Copy, design, build |
| Weeks 3–5 | Profile, tracking, CRM | GBP, conversion tracking, pipeline, routing, workflows |
| Week 5 | Campaign build and system test | Campaign paused; every route tested |
| Week 6 | Acceptance, training, handover | Tests passed, training done, documents delivered, campaign live |

### Acceptance criteria

1. Test form submission appears in the CRM with correct source.
2. Test call is recorded and attributed.
3. Assigned user receives the notification; lead receives the acknowledgement.
4. Test conversions appear in Google Ads.
5. Site and landing page render correctly on an agreed device and browser list.
6. Client holds owner or admin access to every account.
7. Documentation delivered and both training sessions held.

### Training, documentation, handover

Two recorded sessions of about an hour (CRM lead handling; running Google Ads).
System map, account and access register, weekly and monthly operating checklist,
short site-editing and user-admin guides.

### Correction window

30 days from handover. Covers defects against the acceptance criteria. Excludes new
features, changes of direction, third-party outages and platform policy changes.
Consultant access removed at the end unless the client asks otherwise.

### What the client owns afterwards

Ad budget and optimisation (search terms, negatives, bids, ads), CRM operation,
review requests, profile and site updates, subscriptions and renewals.
No promise of revenue, rankings, lead volume, profitability or ROI.

## 4. Price recommendation (pending approval)

**Recommended: $9,500 one-time, fixed. Working range $8,500–$12,000 by scope.**
Suggested terms: 50% at kickoff, 50% at acceptance.

Basis:

- Reported component prices bought separately, low end to mid-range: website
  $3,000–$8,000, ads setup $500–$2,500, CRM build $1,500–$3,500. Landing page and
  profile work add to that. A reported bundled comparable starts at $7,500 plus monthly.
- Estimated delivery effort of 100–125 hours: website 35–45, landing page 10–12,
  profile 4–6, ads and tracking 14–18, CRM 16–20, QA, training and documents 10–12,
  discovery and project management 8–10. At $9,500 that is about $76–$95 per hour.
  This is an estimate, not measured delivery data.

**Assessment fee:** undecided. The site says terms and any fee are sent in writing
first, and nowhere says the assessment is free. Option to consider: a paid assessment
credited against the build.

**Always separate, often recurring:** advertising spend, domain, hosting, CRM
subscription, call tracking, messaging fees and carrier registration, any other software.

Neither the price nor the range appears on the website. Add it only after approval.
