01Who it’s for

Built for established, owner-led home-service companies.

Remodeling, roofing, HVAC, plumbing, electrical and similar trades — businesses that do good work, win mostly on referrals, and want a dependable way to be found and to handle every inquiry properly.

A good fit

  • You have been operating for at least two years and have capacity to take on more work.
  • Each job is worth enough that one missed inquiry matters.
  • A named person will answer new leads during business hours.
  • You can fund advertising yourself, on your own card, after launch.
  • One decision-maker can give feedback within a few business days.
  • You want to own and operate the system, not rent it.

Not a fit

  • You want someone to manage your ads every month. This is a build and a handover.
  • You need a guaranteed number of leads, a ranking or a return. Nobody can honestly promise those.
  • Nobody on your team will open the CRM or return calls.
  • You are pre-launch, or still working out which services you sell.
  • You need e-commerce, a customer portal or custom software.

02What to check

Five places an inquiry usually gets lost.

These are patterns to check, not assumptions about your business. The assessment exists to find out which of them actually apply to you — and to say so plainly if they don’t.

  1. Unclear positioning

    A visitor cannot tell within a few seconds what you do, where you work and why to call you rather than the next result.

  2. A weak conversion path

    Paid clicks land on a general home page with several competing options, instead of one page built around one service and one next step.

  3. Missed inquiries

    Calls ring out while the crew is on a job. Form submissions arrive in an inbox that nobody is responsible for.

  4. Poor tracking

    Money goes to advertising, but nobody can say which calls and forms came from it — so every budget decision is a guess.

  5. Inconsistent follow-up

    Whether a lead hears back, and how quickly, depends on who happened to see it and how busy that day was.

03The Front Door Build

Five components. One system.

Each piece answers one of the problems above. The value is in the connections between them: an ad leads to a page built for it, the page records where the inquiry came from, and the CRM makes sure someone responds.

A

Business website

AddressesUnclear positioning

  • Positioning and messaging brief agreed before any design starts.
  • Up to seven pages, written and designed: home, up to three service pages, about, service area, contact.
  • Responsive on phone, tablet and desktop; built to accessibility and page-speed good practice.
  • On-page basics: titles, descriptions, headings, local business structured data, sitemap.
  • Click-to-call and forms on every page, connected to the CRM.
B

Conversion landing page

AddressesA weak conversion path

  • One dedicated page for your priority service, separate from the main site navigation.
  • One offer, one action: call or request a quote.
  • Copy matched to the search terms and ads that send people to it.
  • Form and call tracking wired to Google Ads and the CRM.
C

Google Business Profile

AddressesBeing found, and trusted, in local results

  • One location claimed or cleaned up: categories, services, service area, hours, description.
  • Your photos uploaded and organised; links and call button checked.
  • Tracked website link so profile visits are distinguishable in analytics.
  • A simple review-request routine your team can run, with message templates.
D

Google Ads account and campaign

AddressesPoor tracking

  • Account created in your name, with your billing; I work as an invited user.
  • Conversion tracking for form submissions and calls, tested end to end before any ad runs.
  • One Search campaign for your priority service: up to three ad groups, keywords, negative keywords, ads and extensions.
  • Location targeting, schedule and a starting daily budget that you approve.
  • A written operating guide for the weekly checks you will run after handover.
E

CRM, lead routing and follow-up

AddressesMissed inquiries and inconsistent follow-up

  • One CRM, on a subscription you hold, chosen at the assessment to suit your team.
  • One pipeline of up to six stages and up to three user accounts.
  • Every form and tracked call creates a lead with its source attached.
  • Routing rules: each new lead is assigned to a named person and they are notified.
  • Up to five workflows, such as instant acknowledgement, missed-call reply, a short follow-up sequence and a stale-lead reminder.
  • One import of existing contacts from a clean spreadsheet.

Outside the scope

  • Ongoing Google Ads management or optimisation after handover.
  • Ongoing SEO, link building, blogging or social media.
  • Logo or brand identity design; photography or video production.
  • E-commerce, booking engines, portals or custom software.
  • Additional locations, campaigns or ad platforms.
  • Legal texts such as your privacy policy and terms.

What you provide

  • One decision-maker, and feedback within three business days at each review.
  • Access to existing accounts, or creation of new ones in your name, in week one.
  • Real photos of your work and team, licence and insurance details, and service information.
  • Your own payment method for advertising and software subscriptions.
  • The people who will handle leads, present at both training sessions.

Costs that are separate, and may recur

The implementation fee covers my work only. You pay third parties directly for advertising spend, domain registration, website hosting, the CRM subscription, call-tracking and text-messaging fees, and any other software you choose. Most of these recur monthly or yearly for as long as you use them. Expected amounts are itemised in your assessment before you decide.

05Handover

Finished means tested, documented and yours.

The project ends when the system passes a written list of acceptance tests — not when I say it is done.

Acceptance tests

  • A test form submission appears in the CRM with the correct source.
  • A test call is recorded and attributed.
  • The assigned person receives the notification; the lead receives the acknowledgement.
  • Test conversions show in Google Ads.
  • Site and landing page display correctly on an agreed list of devices and browsers.
  • You hold owner or admin access to every account.

Training and documents

  • Two live sessions of about an hour, recorded: handling leads in the CRM, and running your Google Ads account.
  • A system map showing how each component connects.
  • An account and access register.
  • A weekly and monthly operating checklist.
  • Short guides for editing the site and adding users.

After handover

  • For 30 days I correct anything that does not work as specified in the acceptance tests.
  • New features and changes of direction are quoted separately.
  • At the end of the window my access is removed, unless you ask me to keep it.

The work that becomes yours

A system like this performs only if someone runs it. After handover, your team is responsible for the advertising budget and its optimisation — reviewing search terms, adding negative keywords, adjusting bids and ads — and for operating the CRM: answering leads, updating stages, requesting reviews and keeping the profile current. I show you how and leave written checklists. I do not promise revenue, rankings, lead volume or a return on your spend; those depend on your market, your budget and how consistently the system is used.

07Questions

Scope, ownership, timing, fees and handover.

What exactly am I buying?

One implementation with five connected parts: a business website of up to seven pages, one conversion landing page, one Google Business Profile, one Google Ads account with one Search campaign and conversion tracking, and one CRM with routing and up to five follow-up workflows. It ends with acceptance tests, two training sessions, written documentation and a 30-day correction window.

Why a one-time build instead of a monthly retainer?

Because the two are different jobs. Building the system is a project with a clear end. Operating it — answering leads, tuning ads — is ongoing work that is best done by people inside your business, or by a specialist you choose later. I do the first and prepare you for the second.

Who owns the website, the accounts and the data?

You do. The domain, hosting, Google Business Profile, Google Ads account, analytics and CRM are created in your name or transferred to it, with your billing details. I work as an invited user and my access is removed at the end of the correction window. Website content and design are yours on final payment.

How long does it take?

The proposed schedule is about six weeks from kickoff to handover. It assumes account access in week one and feedback within three business days at each review. Some steps are outside anyone’s control — Google decides how and when a Business Profile is verified, and text-messaging registration is approved by carriers — so those are started early and flagged if they slip.

What does it cost?

One fixed fee for the implementation, confirmed in writing after the assessment and before you commit to the build. It does not change unless the scope does. Advertising spend, hosting, domains, CRM subscriptions, messaging fees and other third-party costs are separate, paid by you directly, and many recur.

What is the Implementation Assessment, and what happens after I apply?

The assessment is a structured review of how inquiries currently reach and move through your business. I read every application personally. If there appears to be a fit, you receive the assessment’s scope, terms and any fee in writing before you agree to anything. If there is not, I will tell you.

What do I have to keep doing after handover?

Run it. That means responding to leads in the CRM, checking the Google Ads account weekly, paying for your subscriptions and ad spend, asking customers for reviews and keeping your profile and site current. Expect a few hours a week. The operating checklist sets out each task and how often to do it.

Do you guarantee leads, rankings or a return?

No. I guarantee the scope: that what is specified is built, connected and passes its acceptance tests. Lead volume, cost per lead, search position and profitability depend on your market, competition, budget, pricing and follow-up — none of which I control after handover.

I already have a website, a CRM or an ad account. Can you use them?

Often, yes. Existing assets are reviewed in the assessment. A sound website can be improved rather than replaced, an existing ad account keeps its history, and a CRM your team already uses is usually worth keeping. The scope and fee are adjusted in writing to reflect what is reused.

What about automated text messages?

Text follow-up is useful and regulated. Messages are sent only to people who have given consent, and in the United States your business must register its messaging with the carriers before sending. I set up the workflows and guide you through registration; approval and per-message fees are between you and the provider.
